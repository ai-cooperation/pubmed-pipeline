## Abstract

**INTRODUCTION**: Stroke is the second leading cause of death and disability creating a huge economic burden annually. Robot-assisted training (RT) is a promising therapy in stroke rehabilitation, but for the elderly, traditional 'reaching objects'" tasks do not seem to create sufficient motivation, an important factor in rehabilitation, which reduces the effect of rehabilitation. Combining RT with some popular card games like mahjong, a popular tiled-based game in the Chinese elderly, is a strategy to motivate stroke survivors. Combining functional near-infrared imaging spectroscopy technique (fNIRS), the aim of this trial is to explore the effects of mahjong-based RT on stroke survivors compared with traditional RT.

**METHODS AND ANALYSIS**: The three-arm, assessor-blinded, randomised controlled trial will allocate 18 participants in each group, traditional robot-assisted training (TRT) group, mahjong game-based upper limb robot-assisted training (MULR) group and conventional rehabilitation group. Participants in these three groups will receive 30-min physical therapy and 30-min occupational therapy 5 days per week for 3 weeks. Participants in the TRT group will receive an extra 30-min TRT, while participants in the MULR group will receive an extra 30-min MULR 5 days per week for 3 weeks. The primary outcome will be the neuromuscular function of upper limb assessed by Fugl-Meyer Assessment of Upper Extremity assessed at baseline and after the last treatment has been completed. Other outcomes will include cognitive function assessed by Montreal Cognitive Assessment, rehabilitation motivation assessed by Pittsburgh Rehabilitation Participation Scale, activities of daily living assessed by Modified Barthel Index, emotion assessed by self-rating anxiety scale and self-rating depression scale) and brain neural activity assessed by fNIRS. Two-way analysis of variance, Welch's ANOVA, post hoc comparison and simple effects analyses will be used for the analysis of scale data; while generalisation linear model analysis and seed-based correlation will be used for the analysis of fNIRS data.

**ETHICS AND DISSEMINATION**: This trial was approved by the ethics committee of the West China Hospital of Sichuan University, China (reference number: 2024298). The results of this trial will be published in peer-reviewed scientific journals.

**TRIAL REGISTRATION NUMBER**: This trial has been registered on the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry, https://www.chictr.org.cn. The reference number is ChiCTR2400084049. The registered name is 'Effect of mahjong game-based upper limb robot training on upper limb function in stroke patients: a functional near-infrared clinical study protocol'.

### Background and rationale

Stroke is the second leading cause of death and disability worldwide, and it creates a huge economic burden annually.1 One of the most significant challenges faced by stroke survivors is upper limb dysfunction.2 Approximately 80% of stroke survivors experience upper limb impairment; of these, about half have persistent motor deficits,3 which substantially limit their ability to perform activities of daily living. Furthermore, insufficient training intensity and the physical distance to rehabilitation centres pose considerable challenges to effective upper limb rehabilitation for stroke survivors.4

One promising therapy is robot-assisted training (RT) as it could provide cost-effective training and release the labour force even in areas far from rehabilitation centres5; however, for some elderly, the unfamiliarity of RT might induce a lack of motivation and participation, which is related to insufficient interpersonal interaction, poor interests, etc.6 7 While numerous upper limb rehabilitation robots use game-based approaches, the initiative and participation of older adults are often not effectively stimulated, as the game designs frequently overlook their specific interests and capabilities. Traditional card games might be a more efficient medium to motivate stroke survivors as traditional card games are widely receptive and fond in the elderly group. Besides, card games are proven to significantly improve executive function and visual-spatial attention.810 Mahjong, a traditional Chinese tiled-based game, is played with 144 tiles by 4 players, requiring players to take strategy, skill and a certain degree of chance to complete a particular combination of 14 tiles. Thus, combining RT with mahjong seems to provide highly motivated and extra cognitive training to stroke survivors. Although some Chinese companies have integrated mahjong games into upper limb robotic rehabilitation to boost patient motivation, there is currently insufficient clinical evidence to demonstrate their superiority over traditional methods. Consequently, well-designed clinical trials are necessary to establish a more scientific and rational approach to treatment, improve patient engagement and guide effective clinical application.

This study aims to investigate the effects of mahjong game-based upper limb robot-assisted training (MULR) on upper limb motor impairment in stroke patients, and provide new evidence and guidance for future clinical practice and research.

Study design: a three-arm single-blind randomised controlled trial.

### Primary aim

To determine the effect of a 3-week MULR on upper limb motor function (measured by Fugl-Meyer Assessment Upper Extremity Scale (FMA-UE)) in first-ever stroke patients, compared with traditional task-oriented robot-assisted training (TRT) and conventional rehabilitation therapy (CT).

### Secondary aim

To determine the effect of a 3-week MULR on cognitive function, motivation, emotion, basic activities of daily living and neuroimaging changes in first-ever stroke patients.

### Null hypothesis

There is no difference among people in the MULR group, traditional RT group and CT group in upper limb motor function.

### Patient and public involvement

We do not include public or patient involvement in the design or oversight of this study. We acknowledge that patient and public involvement (PPI) is highly valuable. As this specific phase is a technical feasibility study focused on methodology and hardware integration, the opportunities for meaningful PPI in its design were limited. However, our team includes clinical experts to ensure relevance, and we are committed to integrating a PPI panel in the next phase to guide the subsequent clinical translation and dissemination of our findings.

### Trial design and setting

This is an assessor-blinded, three-arm randomised controlled trial consisting of two sessions of evaluation (baseline and endpoint (3 weeks later)) and three groups (MULR, TRT and CT). MULR is the experimental group, TRT is the control group and CT is the passive control group. The allocation ratio of participants is 1:1:1.

Figure 1 shows the flow chart of this trial.

This trial will start on 1 June 2024 in the rehabilitation centre of West China Hospital of Sichuan University, Chengdu, Sichuan Province, China, and will end on 31 June 2026.

### Eligibility criteria

Because MULR requires active movement of the affected limbs to manipulate the cursor, we established upper limb function cut-offs. Given that most functional recovery after stroke occurs within the first 6 months,11 12 we also set the cut-offs for onset time of stroke. In China, mahjong is mainly popular among middle-aged and elderly individuals and the primary population affected by stroke is the elderly and a portion of middle-aged individuals.13 We believe age over 40 years is the group that benefits the most from our intervention. The following criteria are designed to identify participants who are most likely to benefit from the intervention and to minimise potential confounding factors.

Inclusion criteria: (1) patients with first-ever stroke; (2) patients within 1 week to 6 months after the first onset of stroke; (1) and (2) will be identified by combining patient interview, review of electronic health records (EHRs) and brain imaging findings; (3) FMA-UE ≥33; (4) MMSE ≥21; (5) able to keep a static sitting balance; (6) aged from 40 to 80 years.

Exclusion criteria: (1) associated with severe heart, lung, liver, kidney disease or other serious diseases; (2) previous history of seizures or family history of epilepsy; (3) implanted metals in the body like pacemakers; (4) new infarcts or worsening secondary bleeding are detected; (5) severe cardiovascular, pulmonary, hepatic, renal or other major systemic diseases; (6) enrolled in another clinical trial. All the above information will be identified by combining patient interview and review of EHRs.

### Intervention and comparator

All participants will be allocated into three groups: MULR group, TRT group and CT group. Referring to the UK’s guidelines and past upper limb robot studies for poststroke rehabilitation,5 14 15 participants in each group will undergo conventional rehabilitation, with a similar duration and frequency: 30 min physical therapy and 30 min occupational therapy per session, 5 days per week, for 3 weeks. Participants in the MULR group will accept extra MULR; while participants in the TRT group will accept extra TRT. All participants will be required to maintain the original lifestyle and not to seek extra upper limb medical care and therapy. For people with aphasia, low-tech augmentative and alternative communication—such as gestures and communication boards—will be used to prompt engagement, and specific therapists will guide them throughout the process.

### Conventional rehabilitation therapy group (CT group)

Subjects in the CT group will be treated with clinical pharmacotherapy and conventional rehabilitation. Conventional rehabilitation consists of 30-min physical therapy and 30-min occupational therapy per day, 5 days per week, for 3 weeks. Both physical therapy and occupational therapy will be set based on each subject’s functional level (assessed by FMA-UE at baseline) and aim to improve the upper limb function. It will be conducted by well-experienced therapists in the rehabilitation centre. Physical therapy will mainly include muscle strength training and range of motion (ROM) training for the shoulder, elbow, wrist and hand joints; upper limb multijoint coordination training and mental practice for upper limb movement. Occupational therapy will mainly include functional training like reaching objects, grasping, task-oriented training and activity of daily living training including grooming and dressing training.

### Mahjong game-based upper limb robot-assisted training (MULR) group

Subjects in the MULR group will accept clinical pharmacotherapy, conventional rehabilitation and MULR. MULR will be conducted 30 min per session, one session per day, 5 days per week for 3 weeks. The Upper Limb Training and Evaluation System (A6) (Guangzhou Yikang Medical Equipment Industrial Co., LTD) will be used for MULR. The Upper Limb Training and Evaluation System consists of a screen, a laptop and an exoskeleton robot. During MULR, participants’ paralysed upper limb will be fixed on the mechanical arm, and the mechanical arm, driven by the movement of the paralysed upper limb, will move the cursor on the screen to make a series of operations like picking tiles, playing a hand, ‘hu tiles’, etc in the mahjong game. The flexion and extension of the shoulder joint will make the cursor go up and down on the screen, and the adduction and abduction of the shoulder joint will make the cursor go left and right. The flexion and extension of the elbow joint will also make the cursor go left and right, but the extent is less than shoulder adduction and abduction, which is for fine adjustment of the cursor in the horizontal direction. Gripping the bar at the distal end of the robotic arm will make the cursor click the corresponding tile or actions like picking, playing a hand, etc. During the game, the maximum ROM of each joint will be set as the maximum range that can be achieved by the robotic arm. The ‘playing a hand’ bottom will randomly appear on the margin of the screen to encourage participants to use the affected limb. The maximum ROM will be calibrated every week by a trained therapist (QZ) to guarantee the difficulty of game matching to the participants’ function. After winning a game, specific animation and music will be played to provide positive feedback to participants.

### Traditional robot-assisted training (TRT) group

Subjects in the TRT group will be treated by clinical pharmacotherapy, conventional rehabilitation and TRT. TRT will be conducted 30 min per session, one session per day, 5 days per week for 3 weeks to guarantee the same training time as MULR group. The TRT will also be conducted by The Upper Limb Training and Evaluation System (A6). The method of fixing and cursor control will be the same as in the MULR group, but the game will be different. In the TRT group, participants will play ‘whack a mole’. In this game, participants will be required to move the cursor in a certain time to track a mole randomly refreshed on the screen, and then grip the bar at the distal end of the robotic arm to whack the mole. The margin of area where refreshes moles will be identified as the maximum ROM that the participant could reach. The maximum ROM will be calibrated every week by another trained therapist (JZ) to guarantee the difficulty of game matching to the participants’ function. After winning a game, specific animation and music will be played to provide positive feedback to participants.

### Functional near-infrared imaging spectroscopy technique (fNIRS)

Functional near-infrared imaging technology (fNIRS) is an optical, non-invasive neuroimaging technique that measures changes in the concentration of oxyhaemoglobin (HbO) and deoxyhaemoglobin (HbR) in brain tissue after neuronal activation.16 17 Due to its high temporal resolution, moderate spatial resolution, low sensitivity to motion artefacts, allowing movement during test and easy to wear, fNIRS is increasingly used in cerebral function monitoring in stroke experiments.1820 Compared with traditional imaging technology like functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) and electroencephalogram (EEG), fNIRS is applicable when monitoring tasks requiring movement of the body such as rehabilitation. Evidence has demonstrated the feasibility and reliability of using fNIRS to investigate brain function changes following stroke. Stroke patients typically exhibit reduced activation in the affected hemisphere compared with healthy controls, while the unaffected hemispheres often show compensatory increased activity during motor tasks.2125 Additionally, the unaffected hemisphere in stroke patients frequently demonstrates enhanced functional connectivity, particularly in the M1 region.26 In the present study, we will use fNIRS to monitor brain activity in stroke patients at different stages of recovery.

A multichannel fNIRS system (NirSmart-6000, Danyang Huichuang Medical Equipment Co., Ltd., Jiangsu, China) will be used to record the haemodynamic responses. The system consists of 48 effective channels structured by 20 sensors and 20 transmitters. The distance between each transmitter and sensor will be 3 cm and two wavelengths of 730 nm and 850 nm of infrared light will be used at the sampling rate of 11 Hz. Based on the International 10–20 System,27 all transmitters and sensors will be symmetrically fixed on the custom-made cap and worn on the participants’ head. These channels will cover the left primary motor area (L-M1), the right primary motor area (R-M1), bilateral premotor area and supplementary motor area (L-PMC+SMC, R-LMC+PMC), the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (L-DLPFC) and the right dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (R-DLPFC) as shown in figures2  3. ### Data collection methods

Two trained assessors (two well-experienced occupational therapists in the hospital, Chuang Du and Shixing Li) who will not be informed of group allocation will be responsible for all assessments during the whole experiment. ### Sample size

A previous unpublished pilot study, conducting the same interventions, assessment and statistical analysis, with 12 subjects in each group is done to determine the η² and effect size of the primary outcome (FMA-UE). After calculation by R software, the partial η² of group=0.073, and effect size of f=0.281 are determined to be used in the sample size calculation. The following parameters are also used: α=0.05, power=0.90, group of numbers=3, test family=F tests, statistical test=ANOVA: repeated measures, within-between interactions in G*power, a widely-used tool for computing statistical power and sample size,30 31 for the formal experiment’s sample size calculation. The total sample size calculated by G*power is 45. Considering the 20% drop-out rate, the final sample size is determined as 54 with 18 participants in each group.

The pilot study data used in sample size calculation are not publicly available due to privacy/ethical restrictions concerning human participants. Researchers with a reasonable request may contact the corresponding author (xyprotk@126.com) to access de-identified datasets under a data use agreement.

### Recruitment

Subjects will be recruited according to three main mechanisms: (1) posters in the hospital; (2) the recruitment advertisement will be published by Wechat official account of the rehabilitation medicine centre; (3) identification in the HIS system of West China Hospital of Sichuan University. All advertising and posters will be priorly checked by the local ethics committee.

### Randomisation: sequence generation, allocation concealment mechanism and implementation

The random number sequence will be generated by an occupational therapist, Wang Fengyi, on a website (https://www.randomizer.org/). Participants will be randomly allocated to the CT group, the MULR group and the TRT group with a 1:1:1 proportion.

Non-transparent, sealed envelopes will be numbered by continuous numbers for the allocation concealment. In each envelope, a paper with ‘CT group’, ‘MULR group’ or ‘TRT group’” will be contained according to the corresponding allocation of the number. After recruitment and initial assessment, a therapist (HT) will open the envelope and find the treatment condition of the patient. HT will then inform the patient about the allocation and treatment.

### Blinding

The staff responsible for the recruitment, assessment and statistical analysis will not be informed about the group allocation during the experiment.

### Statistical methods

All data will be analysed by Statistical Package for the Social Science (SPSS) V.29.0 software. The analyser will be blinded to the group allocation. The fNIRS analysis will be conducted by NirSpark toolbox and SPSS.

Mean value with SD will be used to present normally distributed variables, and medians with interquartile ranges will be used to present non-normally distributed variables. Proportion and frequency will be used to show the categorical variable.

To assess the brain activation in different states, task-related haemodynamics at the individual level will be modelled using the generalisation linear model (GLM) analysis based on the relative HBO concentration. In this trial, a GLM will be constructed based on the NirSpark toolbox and the β features of the fNIRS signal will be extracted. After the model is established, the β values of each signal channel of each subject will be extracted as the data of neural activity changes. The mean β values of all signal channels in the brain region of a single subject will be defined as the activation intensity of the brain region. One-way ANOVA with Bonferroni multiple comparisons will be performed for data conforming to a normal distribution; while Welch’s ANOVA and Tambane’s T2 test will be performed for the comparison of non-normally distributed data. The p<0.05 will be defined as the significant difference level; p<0.001 will be defined as a highly significant difference level.

Functional connectivity (FC): FC refers to the temporal correlation or dependence of neural activity among various regions of the cerebral cortex, which is used to measure the degree of functional coupling between brain regions. In this trial, we will pick the M1 as the seed area to perform the time series correlation analysis of ΔHBO signal to calculate the Pearson’s correlation coefficient between M1 and other channels. Then we will use the seed-correlation method to construct the FC map of the whole brain. After which, the connectivity matrix for each subject will be transformed to Z map by Fisher-Z transformation. Consequently, the correlation coefficient will be calculated between ROI and other regions. Finally, the Pearson correlation coefficients of the three groups at the group data level will be analysed by one-way ANOVA, and the results will be corrected by FDR to explore whether there are statistical differences in functional connectivity at the ROI/channel level.

After the primary endpoint assessment, all outcome assessors independently completed a postassessment questionnaire in which they guessed each participant’s group assignment (CT, MULR, TRT or ‘Don’t Know’). Guessed assignments will be compared with actual group allocations to derive each assessor’s correct-guess rate. We will use a one-sided binomial test (α=0.05) to assess whether the correct-guess rate significantly exceeded the chance level (33.3%).

The intention-to-treat principle will be used in all statistical analyses. According to the principle, all participants will be regarded as randomised in the statistical analysis regardless of what intervention they actually received. The missing data will be imputed by multiple imputations, which will be based on five replications and the Markov chain Monte Carlo method in the SAS MI procedure.
